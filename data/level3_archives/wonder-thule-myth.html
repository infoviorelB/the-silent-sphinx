Modern scholarship often debates Pytheas's veracity, yet his account spurred numerous expeditions, each pursuing the mythic Thule as though it were a tangible reality.

Some archivists suggest there is more to Thule than a simple geographical pursuit; a declassified document from the mid-20th century hints at Thule being a metaphor for an Aryan utopia, an idea appropriated by the Thule Society. What modern minds often overlook are the peculiarities surrounding the Thule myth. One such enigma is the recurrence of a mysterious substance referred to by Pytheas as "marine lung," a gelatinous mixture of air and water that defies explanation. Some hypothesize it to be sea ice or slush, yet this unproven detail adds a curious layer to the narrative, challenging those who decipher the boundaries between fact and fiction.
