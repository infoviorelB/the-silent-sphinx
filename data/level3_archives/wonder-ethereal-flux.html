The mystical concept of the tetractys, a triangular figure composed of ten points arranged in four rows, was considered by the Pythagoreans as the key to understanding the universe. This arrangement was said to hold within it the secret of the cosmos, a sacred symbol that bridged the material and the immaterial.

Consider the platonic solids—five perfectly symmetrical three-dimensional forms described by Plato as the building blocks of the cosmos in his work "Timaeus." Each element of the natural world was believed to correspond to these shapes. Earth aligned with the cube, air with the octahedron, water with the icosahedron, fire with the tetrahedron, and the universe itself with the dodecahedron. While the ancients enshrined these forms in philosophy, modern science too finds echoes of these shapes in the microscopic and cosmic scales, from the molecular architecture to the structure of space itself.
